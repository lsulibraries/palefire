Where they came from

The eleven exhibitions of the T. Harry Williams Center for Oral History were the old listening stations at lib.lsu.edu/sites/all/files/oralhistory/presentations/<folder>. Each now lives at /v2/oralhistory/exhibitions/<folder>, the old folder’s name in lowercase, so the old and new addresses are easy to match.

Text

  • Carried over word for word, then corrected only where listed in each exhibition’s Corrected list.
  • Text the old pages had saved in the wrong character encoding (letters such as “DufourÃÂ¢s”) was decoded back to the original characters exactly.
  • Straight quotes and apostrophes became curly ones (’ “ ”), and date ranges use an en dash (1939–1942). These are not listed one by one.
  • Transcripts: each speaker’s turn starts a new paragraph, with the speaker’s full name in bold capitals (as on the Mossville History Project), not a first name or initials.
  • Captions come from each exhibition’s own Pictures or credits pages where they had them, since those give fuller citations than the slides did.
  • Every image has new alt text written for people who use screen readers. Alt text is listed under New wording to check.

Audio

Every clip was prepared the same way, so all the exhibitions sound alike and none is noticeably quieter than another (scripts/palefire_exhibit_media.py):

  • Mono. Speech needs only one channel, and a mono file is half the size. A stereo file whose second channel is silent or much quieter (20 dB or more) keeps its live channel instead of being mixed down at half volume.
  • Loudness −16 LUFS (integrated), measured and corrected in two passes, with peaks held under −1.5 dBTP and a loudness range of 11 LU. This is close to what podcasts use. It was −18 LUFS until Mon 16 Nov 2026, when it was raised so the clips aren’t quieter than other sites’ audio. Browsers don’t level volume between sites.
  • AAC at 64 kbps, 44.1 kHz, in .m4a files. This is plenty for speech, plays in every current browser, and keeps files small, so clips start quickly on a phone.
  • Read-along timing. Each clip has a WebVTT timing file beside it, so its transcript lights up phrase by phrase as it plays and a click on a phrase plays from there. The timing was matched to the published transcript, whose words are always the ones shown. There are 230 files for the ten newer exhibitions, plus Mossville’s.
  • A few old pages played the wrong recording for a clip. Those are noted in their exhibition’s list, with the clip that now plays.

Images

  • Masters at 2,560 pixels on the long side (never enlarged), sRGB JPEG at quality 85, with camera and editing metadata removed.
  • On the page, each image is served as WebP at the size the screen needs (640, 1,280, 1,920 or 2,560 pixels wide) and loads only as it comes into view.
