# British Voices from South Asia: migration corrections

Exhibition: British Voices from South Asia, held in Hill Memorial Library, Louisiana State University, April 8 to August 6, 1996. Curated and edited by Rosan Augusta Jordan and Frank de Caro. It marked the T. Harry Williams Center for Oral History’s acquisition of their taped interviews with British people who lived and worked in India before Independence.
Old site: the 1996 web version at /special/exhibits/india/, copied to thirteen Showcase pages under /BritishVoices (node/315–327): an Introduction, six history sections and six pages of interview excerpts.
New page: `/v2/special/exhibitions/british-voices`, built by `scripts/palefire_exhibit_british_voices.php` from `scripts/data/british-voices.json`, unpublished.

## Changed on the page

- Thirteen galleries, every one a wall with a lead picture. Each history section is followed by its own interview excerpts, which the old site kept as six pages at the end (its introduction says “from each section the viewer can move to excerpts related to that section”).
- History sections: the interviewees’ quotations that opened each old page are the gallery’s opening text over the lead. The section essay is a text plate with a new heading and a written summary. Every picture follows as an entry, titled from its caption, with the 1996 caption and the words that followed it on the old page. Pictures that shared one caption (the voyage postcards, the servant postcards, the figurines, *Behind the Bungalow*) are one entry each, as an image group.
- Interview galleries: walls of text plates, each a few speakers’ excerpts in the old order with a new heading and a written summary (42 plates across six galleries). The old pages’ own topic headings (The Voyage Out, Two Great Armies, They Lived in the 16th Century: Managing the Princes, On the Verandah, Behind the Bungalow, “Quite a Fantastic Thing”: Protocol and the Social Whirl, The Warrant of Precedence, “Never Eat the Fish at Moghul-Serai”: Danger, Disease and Death, The Cool of the Hills, “A Nice Little Country Estate”) start sub-galleries. Each speaker’s name is a subheading in the plate.
- Introduction: a wall of three plates, A colonial subculture (the introduction), The exhibition, and Acknowledgments. The old paragraph on moving between sections and enlarging images is left out; it described the old site.
- Leads: each history section opens on the picture the Showcase page used as its cover (Ranjit Singh’s cavalcade, the Junior Naval and Military Club Christmas card, the dog keepers aquatint, paying off the beaters, the Viceroy’s garden party, the family photo album). The Introduction opens on “Christmas in India” (1881); each interview gallery opens on a picture from its section (a voyage postcard, the Superintendent of the Hill States, the Ranchi bungalow, a dandi, the maharajah’s dinner tent, the album’s tea plantation page). Every lead is shown whole (“Show whole image”, framed over a blur of itself): no picture of this exhibition is 1280 × 720 or larger, the 1996 files being 600 px at most, so none is cropped and enlarged.
- Cover: the Survey of India officer mapping in the Himalayas (1930s), lent by Major General R.C.A. Edge, one of the interviewees, shown whole with its object label (“Frame the cover”, as the photograph is 600 px, under 1280 × 720).
- Gallery titles keep the old page titles. “No More India to Go to” and “No India to go to” are capitalized as titles; “Interviews: Quite Improbable Places: Indian Travel” is shortened to “Interviews: Quite Improbable Places”.

## Corrected

- The Introduction: “on July 10, 1947” → “on August 14 and 15, 1947” (the days Pakistan and India became independent).
- The Benares and Calcutta burning ghat postcards had each other’s captions; each now has its own (the Calcutta card is printed “The Burning ghat – CALCUTTA”).
- The caption that held two pictures (“Mysore Government Offices, Cubbon Park” and “View of Calcutta from the Esplanade”) is split between them.
- “Samuel Hewitt” and “Samuel Hewett” → “Samuel Howitt” (the aquatints after Captain Thomas Williamson).
- “Mortimer Menpe7s” → “Mortimer Menpes”
- “memsahinbs” → “memsahibs”
- “fortifiations” → “fortifications”
- “often strained relation” → “often strained relations”
- “19xx” → “1924” (the British Empire Exhibition, Wembley)
- In the interviews: “l93O” → “1930”, “famiy” → “family”, “Byderabad” → “Hyderabad”, “blase’” → “blasé”.
- Sir William Jones’s caption: the sentence about his work on Sanskrit is the entry’s text, not part of the caption.
- Captions are no longer set wholly in italics; titles of books stay italic.
- Curly quotes and apostrophes, em dashes for the transcripts’ double hyphens, en dashes in year ranges (1857–58), ’38 and Bakari ’Id as elisions.
