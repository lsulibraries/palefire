Where they came from

  • AVES (2012) and Occult Science and Philosophy in the Renaissance (2011) were Hill Online Exhibitions on exhibitions.blogs.lib.lsu.edu, a blog that is gone. Their text comes from the Wayback Machine’s copies.
  • John James Audubon in Louisiana (2021) was at lib.lsu.edu/exhibitions/john-james-audubon. The Wayback Machine has its landing and section pages; the bird and manuscript pages come from the students’ copy at /audubon.
  • LSU Union Art Gallery was a six-page showcase on libstage, never on the public site.

Each now lives at /v2/special/exhibitions/<name>, as one page of galleries. Each is built by a script from a data file in the theme (scripts/data/<name>.json), so it can be rebuilt, and every correction is made in the data file as well as on the page.

Text

  • Carried over word for word, then corrected only where listed in each exhibition’s Corrected list.
  • Text the old pages had saved in the wrong character encoding was decoded back to the original characters exactly.
  • Straight quotes and apostrophes became curly ones (’ “ ”), and spaced hyphens used as dashes became dashes. These are not listed one by one.
  • “Middleton Library” became “Main Library”.
  • Links between the old pages point at the new galleries and entries. Links to other sites open in a new tab and say so.
  • Every image has new alt text written for people who use screen readers. Alt text is listed under New wording to check.

Layout

  • Galleries take the place of the old pages, each with a lead image and a number in the contents.
  • Entries are the people, books, birds and shows inside a gallery, each with its own heading and anchor, so other pages can link to it.
  • Walls show a gallery’s images side by side at their own proportions, each opening full screen with its caption and text (Audubon’s editions and drawings, all of the Union Art Gallery).
  • Catalog records are set apart from the text, and readings are typeset as covers linked to the catalog.

Images

  • Each original (4K where there was one) is imported once into the media library, named after its exhibition, so an image used twice is one item and a better scan can replace it everywhere at once.
  • On the page, each image is served as WebP at the size the screen needs (640, 1,280, 1,920 or 2,560 pixels wide) and loads only as it comes into view.
