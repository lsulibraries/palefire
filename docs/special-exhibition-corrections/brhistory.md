# From Red Stick to River Capital: Three Centuries of Baton Rouge History: migration corrections

Exhibition: From Red Stick to River Capital: Three Centuries of Baton Rouge History, shown in Hill Memorial Library, March 8 – July 2, 1999, curated by V. Faye Phillips, with materials from the Louisiana and Lower Mississippi Valley Collections and University Archives.
Old site: the 1999 static pages at /sites/all/files/sc/exhibitions/e-exhibits/redstick, copied to fourteen Showcase pages under /brhistory (nodes 289, 328–340). The text is taken from the Showcase pages and checked against the 1999 pages.
New page: `/v2/special/exhibitions/brhistory`, built by `scripts/palefire_exhibit_brhistory.php` from `scripts/data/brhistory.json`. Built unpublished.

## Changed on the page

- Fourteen galleries, one per old page: Introduction, the twelve cases (Native Americans and Colonial Origins through City Politics) and Other Photographs.
- Every gallery is a wall with a lead picture. The long text is divided into text plates between the pictures; the text is unchanged except for the corrections below, and the plate titles and summaries are new.
- Each case ends with an “In the case” plate listing everything shown in 1999, pictured here or not. Other Photographs ends with “Not pictured”.
- Pictures: the old pages had about 40 small images (250–700px). Where the Louisiana Digital Library holds the same item, or the same view, the new page uses the LDL master instead (rendered to 2,560px at most); the rest are the 1999 site’s own files, which are larger than the Showcase copies. Six pictures are new, each tied to something on the page: the Lytle view of Baton Rouge after the 1862 attack, the Lafayette Street streetcar, Hill Memorial Library in 1905, the Tunnard carriage factory, Lora Hinton (in place of his letterman plaque), and the Standard Oil postcard and 1904 Capitol postcard the case lists named but didn’t picture.
- Lead pictures: every gallery opens on an LDL master, mostly from Andrew D. Lytle’s Baton Rouge photographs. Thirteen are 720p or larger (1,500 × 1,221px for the Pintado plan, 2,560px wide for the rest) and fill the screen. Histories of Baton Rouge opens on Lytle’s 1861 Front Street panorama, which exists only at 1,000 × 382px; nothing larger shows the old riverfront, so it is the best lead there is, and “Show whole image” is ticked: it is shown whole over a blurred copy of itself instead of enlarged.
- The cover is Lytle’s State Capitol (lsu-sc-p15140coll12:267), 1,827 × 2,560px, so “Frame the cover” is left off. The script frames any cover, and shows whole any lead, under 720p (long side under 1,280px or short side under 720px).
- Credits move to the end of the Introduction, with the Louisiana Endowment for the Humanities.
